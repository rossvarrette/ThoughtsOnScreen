## Social and legal concerns with always-on recording

### Takeaway
Always-on devices (Bee, Omi, Friend) record bystanders without asking. In all-party-consent states such as California, that creates real legal exposure for the wearer. Lawyers say the question of whether transcription without stored audio counts as a recording is largely untested. Tennessee is a one-party-consent state, so a wearer who takes part in the conversation is generally covered there, but recording conversations the wearer is not part of is a felony. Push-to-record devices (Plaud, Sandbar) lower the risk.

### Cited Findings
- **Tennessee:** under § 39-13-601(b)(5), a participant (or someone acting with a party's prior consent) may record, unless the purpose is criminal or tortious. Intercepting a conversation you are not part of, with no party's consent, is a Class D felony (§ 39-13-602). — [RCFP Tennessee guide](https://www.rcfp.org/reporters-recording-guide/tennessee/); [ezel.ai summary](https://ezel.ai/surveys/call-conversation-recording-consent-requirements/tennessee). One low-quality site wrongly calls Tennessee all-party. — [allowedhere (unreliable)](https://allowedhere.pages.dev/legality/recording-conversations/tennessee/)
- For always-on wearables, the implication is that a wearer who leaves the device running while away, or that picks up nearby conversations the wearer is not part of, could fall outside one-party protection. — inference from the statutes above.
- **California** (all-party): Penal Code §632 bars recording a confidential conversation without all parties' consent. SB 1130 (introduced Feb 2026) would set criminal penalties for some uses of wearable recorders. It was still pending in the sources found. — [Layer3Labs lawsuits guide](https://www.layer3labs.io/guides/ai-note-taker-lawsuits); [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/california-bill-address-wearable-recording-devices-including-glasses?amp)
- Colin Zick of Foley Hoag (Oct 23, 2026, via CNBC) said broad state wiretap laws could apply even when no audio file is stored, and that wearers who transcribe others without consent could face liability. He called the question largely untested. — [Foley Hoag](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/media-center/2026/october/new-ai-devices-are-pushing-legal-limits-and-could-put-users-in-jeopardy/)
- Related lawsuits: class actions against Meta over Ray-Ban glasses footage sent to contractors (Mar 2026, N.D. Cal.) and recording without the wake word (Oct 2026, proposed California class). AI note-taker suits against Otter.ai (consolidated), Granola (CIPA/ECPA), and Fireflies (BIPA). **I found no ruling yet holding a pendant wearer liable.** — [Layer3Labs](https://www.layer3labs.io/guides/ai-note-taker-lawsuits); [Stearns v. ### Inferences
- For a Tennessee user recording their own meetings, any of these devices is lawful as a participant. Risk rises when traveling or calling into all-party states (CA, FL, WA, PA, IL, MD, MA, etc.) and when always-on devices capture conversations the wearer is not part of.
